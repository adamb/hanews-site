OpenWrt 25.12: what's new, and should your smart-home router upgrade?

HA News · 16 Nov 2026

If your router runs OpenWrt 24.10, its clock is out. OpenWrt stopped promising security updates for 24.10 after September 2026, and the current release, 25.12.6 (18 October 2026), patches holes in LuCI's web server that anyone can poke at without a password. Short answer: upgrade to 25.12.6. Here is what changes, and how to do it without losing your setup.

The 25.12 series is nicknamed "Dave's Guitar", after the late Dave Täht, the bufferbloat fighter whose work made home networks feel snappier for millions of people. 25.12.0 landed on 5 March 2026 with more than 4,700 commits.

The one change that will bite you: apk replaces opkg

The package manager is new. OpenWrt dropped its unmaintained opkg fork for apk, the Alpine Package Keeper. Most package names are the same, but the commands differ: opkg install becomes apk add. Any old script or forum snippet that says opkg will fail. Keep the official opkg-to-apk cheatsheet open the first time.

The one change you will love: upgrades keep your packages

Attended Sysupgrade (ASU) now ships by default. It builds a fresh firmware image with every package you have installed, flashes it, and keeps your settings. No more choosing between "keep settings but reinstall everything" and "clean flash and start over". Bigger-flash devices also get owut, a command-line version.

Also new

  • Shell history now survives between logins (kept in RAM, so it doesn't wear out flash).
  • Wi-Fi scripts were rewritten in ucode, which OpenWrt says is faster and less error-prone than shell.
  • More than 180 new devices, for over 2,200 in total. Most 24.10 devices are still supported. Linux kernel 6.12 throughout.

Why 25.12.6 matters

The 25.12.6 notes say "updating is strongly recommended", and the list explains why. uhttpd, the web server behind LuCI, gets fixes for a batch of flaws reachable without logging in, including a type confusion in /ubus/call/, request smuggling and a Basic Auth realm bypass. There are also fixes for Wi-Fi 7 multi-link frames that could crash hostapd (see hostapd advisory 2026-1), two rpcd privilege escalations for routers with limited LuCI users, a critical luci-app-ser2net bug, and updated Mbed TLS, OpenSSL and wolfSSL.

Your router guards the network your door locks, cameras and Home Assistant box live on. An IoT VLAN helps, but the router itself should not be running unpatched code.

How to upgrade safely

  1. Back up first. LuCI: System → Backup/Flash.
  2. Use Attended Sysupgrade. On 24.10 it is optional, so install luci-app-attendedsysupgrade (or owut) first. It requests an image with your packages and flashes it, keeping settings.
  3. Or use the Firmware Selector to build a custom image for your model. Our Firmware Selector guide walks through it.

Three gotchas from the release notes:

  • From 23.05? The notes say a direct sysupgrade to 25.12 is not officially supported. The safe route is to step through 24.10 first, or do a clean flash.
  • Banana Pi BPI-R4: interfaces were renamed (eth1 to sfp-lan or lan4, eth2 to sfp-wan). You have to upgrade without saving the configuration.
  • Cron logging: if you set cronloglevel, it should be 7 for normal logging, which is now the default.

Going from 24.10, the notes say the upgrade "should be transparent on most devices": settings are translated on first boot.
