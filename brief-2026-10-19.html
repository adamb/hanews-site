01 · ESPHome

ESPHome 2026.10.0b5 ships overnight — the beta pause breaks, the stable moves closer

What happened: Friday's brief read the four-beta plateau as the train slowing. It wasn't: 2026.10.0b5 tagged at 01:03 UTC this morning with 24 PRs. Notables: the recommended ESP-IDF bumps to 5.5.6, tcp_uart/uart_tcp expose connection entities as new platforms, usb_uart gains interface_class and port_number (new feature), mDNS now advertises the connected interface in the TXT record, and MQTT topic matching moved from per-character recursion to a loop. Two breaking changes remain banked for the eventual .0: build-time flash tables for select options and light effects, and remote_base protocol methods no longer virtual.

Why it matters: Five betas in twelve days usually means the stable is days out. If you maintain external components that touch selects, lights, or IR protocols, this is your window to test against the beta before 2026.10.0 lands. The ESP-IDF 5.5.6 bump is also the kind that changes which toolchain your next compile pulls.

02 · Home Assistant

Core 2026.10.2 is live — released Friday, now the stable default

What happened: Core 2026.10.2 released Friday 16 October at 20:04 UTC, and this morning version.home-assistant.io/stable.json advertises it as the default across the current HAOS boards. It's a patch release, mostly fixes: MQTT device triggers and tags no longer fire twice after reload, the group integration no longer crashes when a group has no members, Tesla Fleet redacts schedule coordinates in diagnostics and fixes an IndexError on out-of-range heater levels, KNX no longer cuts off SQLite telegram-store initialization on a timeout, and aiohttp bumps to 3.14.5.

Why it matters: The MQTT double-fire and empty-group crash are both the silent kind of bug — automations triggering twice or sensors going unavailable for no obvious reason. If you saw either on 2026.10.1, updating is warranted; otherwise it's routine patch.

Primary source

03 · Matter & Thread / Ubiquiti

UniFi Network 11 puts Matter devices and Thread mesh into its topology view

What happened: Ubiquiti's UniFi Network 11 lets you pair Matter-over-Thread devices by their pairing codes, then shows manufacturer, model, firmware version, battery and wireless signal strength where the device reports it. Thread devices appear in the same topology view as your Ethernet and Wi-Fi clients — with their Thread border routers as the intermediate hops — and the Time Machine feature keeps historical topology, so you can look back at the mesh state just before a device went dark. UniFi is positioning this as monitoring and diagnostics only: no automations, no device control.

Why it matters: "My Matter device is unresponsive" has always lacked a network-level answer. Home Assistant's Matter 9 server has a good Thread map, but it doesn't keep history. UniFi's Time Machine view — what the mesh looked like before the failure — is the feature Thread troubleshooting has been missing, and for anyone already running UniFi switching gear it's free.
